Abstract

This paper presents a systematic investigation of advanced metaheuristic optimization techniques for solving Optimal Power Flow (OPF) problems across standard IEEE test systems with integrated Flexible AC Transmission Systems (FACTS) devices and Artificial Intelligence (AI) methodologies. The research examines five prominent bio-inspired algorithms—Genetic Algorithm (GA), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Firefly Algorithm (FA), and Bacterial Foraging Optimization (BFO)—applied to IEEE 9-bus, 14-bus, 30-bus, 69-bus, and 118-bus test systems. The study focuses on optimizing multiple objectives including generation cost minimization, transmission loss reduction, voltage profile improvement, and system stability enhancement while integrating various FACTS controllers (SVC, TCSC, UPFC, and DPFC) to address the challenges posed by renewable energy penetration. The research develops novel hybrid AI-enhanced optimization frameworks that combine the exploratory advantages of metaheuristic algorithms with the predictive capabilities of machine learning and deep learning techniques to improve convergence characteristics and solution quality. Extensive comparative analyses across test systems of increasing complexity provide insights into algorithm performance, computational efficiency, and effectiveness of FACTS placement strategies under different operating conditions. The findings demonstrate significant improvements in power system performance metrics while establishing practical guidelines for optimal deployment of FACTS devices using AI-integrated optimization approaches in modern power systems with high renewable energy penetration.

Keywords: Bio-inspired optimization, FACTS devices, Optimal power flow, Artificial intelligence, Renewable energy integration, Metaheuristic algorithms, Multi-objective optimization

1. Introduction

The modern power system landscape is undergoing unprecedented transformation driven by the increasing integration of renewable energy sources, deregulation of electricity markets, and growing demand for reliable and efficient power delivery. This evolution has introduced new challenges including voltage stability issues, power quality concerns, and transmission congestion problems that traditional power system control methods struggle to address effectively.

Flexible AC Transmission Systems (FACTS) devices have emerged as crucial technologies for addressing these challenges by providing dynamic control of power system parameters including voltage magnitude, phase angle, and impedance. These devices, including Static VAR Compensators (SVC), Thyristor Controlled Series Capacitors (TCSC), Unified Power Flow Controllers (UPFC), and Distributed Power Flow Controllers (DPFC), offer unprecedented flexibility in power system operation and control.

However, determining the optimal location, sizing, and control settings of FACTS devices presents a complex multi-objective optimization problem that cannot be solved effectively using conventional optimization techniques. The highly nonlinear, non-convex, and multi-modal nature of the optimal power flow (OPF) problem with FACTS devices necessitates the application of advanced optimization methodologies.

Bio-inspired metaheuristic algorithms have demonstrated remarkable success in solving complex optimization problems across various engineering domains. These algorithms, inspired by natural phenomena and biological processes, offer robust global search capabilities that are well-suited for tackling the challenges posed by FACTS device optimization in power systems.

2. Mathematical Formulation

2.1 Multi-Objective Optimal Power Flow Problem

The multi-objective OPF problem with FACTS devices can be formulated as:

$$\min F(x) = [f_1(x), f_2(x), f_3(x), f_4(x)]$$

Subject to:

$$g(x) = 0 \quad \text{(equality constraints)}$$ $$h(x) \leq 0 \quad \text{(inequality constraints)}$$

2.1.1 Objective Functions

Generation Cost Minimization:

$$f_1(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_G} (a_i + b_i P_{Gi} + c_i P_{Gi}^2)$$

Transmission Loss Minimization:

$$f_2(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N_L} g_k [V_i^2 + V_j^2 - 2V_i V_j \cos(\delta_i - \delta_j)]$$

Voltage Profile Improvement:

$$f_3(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_B} (V_i - V_{i,ref})^2$$

System Stability Enhancement:

$$f_4(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_G} \sum_{j=1}^{N_L} S_{ij}^2$$

2.1.2 Constraints

Power Balance Equations:

$$P_{Gi} - P_{Di} - V_i \sum_{j=1}^{N_B} V_j [G_{ij}\cos(\delta_i - \delta_j) + B_{ij}\sin(\delta_i - \delta_j)] = 0$$ $$Q_{Gi} - Q_{Di} - V_i \sum_{j=1}^{N_B} V_j [G_{ij}\sin(\delta_i - \delta_j) - B_{ij}\cos(\delta_i - \delta_j)] = 0$$

Generator Limits:

$$P_{Gi}^{min} \leq P_{Gi} \leq P_{Gi}^{max}$$ $$Q_{Gi}^{min} \leq Q_{Gi} \leq Q_{Gi}^{max}$$

Voltage Limits:

$$V_i^{min} \leq V_i \leq V_i^{max}$$

Line Flow Limits:

$$S_{ij} \leq S_{ij}^{max}$$

3. FACTS Device Mathematical Models

3.1 Static VAR Compensator (SVC)

The SVC is modeled as a variable susceptance connected at bus i:

$$P_{SVC} = 0$$ $$Q_{SVC} = -V_i^2 B_{SVC}$$ $$B_{SVC}^{min} \leq B_{SVC} \leq B_{SVC}^{max}$$

3.2 Thyristor Controlled Series Capacitor (TCSC)

The TCSC is modeled as a variable reactance in series with the transmission line:

$$X_{ij} = X_{line} + X_{TCSC}$$ $$X_{TCSC}^{min} \leq X_{TCSC} \leq X_{TCSC}^{max}$$

3.3 Unified Power Flow Controller (UPFC)

The UPFC combines series and shunt compensation capabilities:

$$P_{sh} = V_i^2 G_{sh} - V_i V_{sh} [G_{sh}\cos(\delta_i - \delta_{sh}) + B_{sh}\sin(\delta_i - \delta_{sh})]$$ $$Q_{sh} = -V_i^2 B_{sh} - V_i V_{sh} [G_{sh}\sin(\delta_i - \delta_{sh}) - B_{sh}\cos(\delta_i - \delta_{sh})]$$

3.4 Distributed Power Flow Controller (DPFC)

The DPFC extends UPFC capabilities with distributed series converters:

$$P_{se,k} = V_k V_{se,k} [G_{se,k}\cos(\delta_k - \delta_{se,k}) + B_{se,k}\sin(\delta_k - \delta_{se,k})]$$ $$Q_{se,k} = V_k V_{se,k} [G_{se,k}\sin(\delta_k - \delta_{se,k}) - B_{se,k}\cos(\delta_k - \delta_{se,k})]$$

4. Bio-Inspired Metaheuristic Algorithms

4.1 Genetic Algorithm (GA)

Algorithm Steps:

  1. Initialization: Generate random population of size N
  2. Evaluation: Calculate fitness for each individual
  3. Selection: Select parents using tournament/roulette wheel selection
  4. Crossover: Apply crossover with probability Pc
  5. Mutation: Apply mutation with probability Pm
  6. Replacement: Replace population with offspring
  7. Termination: Check stopping criteria

Crossover Operation:

$$child_1 = \alpha \cdot parent_1 + (1-\alpha) \cdot parent_2$$ $$child_2 = (1-\alpha) \cdot parent_1 + \alpha \cdot parent_2$$

4.2 Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

Algorithm Steps:

  1. Initialization: Initialize particles with random positions and velocities
  2. Evaluation: Evaluate fitness of each particle
  3. Update Personal Best: Update pbest for each particle
  4. Update Global Best: Update gbest for the swarm
  5. Velocity Update: Update particle velocities
  6. Position Update: Update particle positions
  7. Termination: Check stopping criteria

Update Equations:

$$v_i^{t+1} = w \cdot v_i^t + c_1 \cdot r_1 \cdot (p_{best,i} - x_i^t) + c_2 \cdot r_2 \cdot (g_{best} - x_i^t)$$ $$x_i^{t+1} = x_i^t + v_i^{t+1}$$

4.3 Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)

Algorithm Steps:

  1. Initialization: Initialize pheromone trails
  2. Solution Construction: Each ant constructs a solution probabilistically
  3. Pheromone Update: Update pheromone trails based on solution quality
  4. Evaporation: Apply pheromone evaporation
  5. Termination: Check stopping criteria

Probability Selection:

$$P_{ij} = \frac{[\tau_{ij}]^\alpha \cdot [\eta_{ij}]^\beta}{\sum_{k \in allowed} [\tau_{ik}]^\alpha \cdot [\eta_{ik}]^\beta}$$

4.4 Firefly Algorithm (FA)

Algorithm Steps:

  1. Initialization: Generate random firefly population
  2. Brightness Calculation: Calculate brightness based on objective function
  3. Movement: Move fireflies toward brighter ones
  4. Random Movement: Apply random movement to best firefly
  5. Termination: Check stopping criteria

Movement Equation:

$$x_i = x_i + \beta_0 e^{-\gamma r_{ij}^2}(x_j - x_i) + \alpha (\text{rand} - 0.5)$$

4.5 Bacterial Foraging Optimization (BFO)

Algorithm Steps:

  1. Chemotaxis: Bacteria move based on nutrient gradient
  2. Swarming: Bacteria attract each other during foraging
  3. Reproduction: Healthier bacteria split, others die
  4. Elimination-Dispersal: Random bacteria are eliminated and dispersed

Chemotactic Movement:

$$\theta^i(j+1,k,l) = \theta^i(j,k,l) + C(i) \frac{\Delta(i)}{\sqrt{\Delta^T(i)\Delta(i)}}$$

5. AI-Enhanced Optimization Frameworks

5.1 Machine Learning Integration

The integration of machine learning techniques enhances the performance of bio-inspired algorithms through:

  • Surrogate Modeling: Neural networks approximate power flow solutions for faster evaluation
  • Parameter Adaptation: Deep learning networks predict optimal algorithm parameters
  • Solution Prediction: ML models guide search toward promising regions
  • Convergence Enhancement: AI techniques improve convergence characteristics

5.2 Neural-Enhanced Genetic Algorithm (NEGA)

NEGA integrates a recurrent neural network (RNN) to predict promising search directions:

$$h_t = \tanh(W_{hh}h_{t-1} + W_{xh}x_t + b_h)$$ $$y_t = W_{hy}h_t + b_y$$

The RNN output guides the genetic operators, improving exploration efficiency.

5.3 Deep Reinforcement Learning-PSO (DRL-PSO)

DRL-PSO uses deep Q-networks to adapt particle movement strategies:

$$Q(s,a) = \mathbb{E}[R_{t+1} + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s_{t+1}, a') | s_t = s, a_t = a]$$

The DQN learns optimal velocity update strategies based on swarm state feedback.

5.4 Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Firefly Algorithm (ANFFA)

ANFFA employs an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) to adjust FA parameters:

$$f = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i f_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i}$$

Where wi are the firing strengths of fuzzy rules, dynamically adjusted based on algorithm state.

8. Multi-Objective Optimization and Pareto Analysis

8.1 Pareto Front Analysis

The multi-objective nature of the FACTS placement problem requires careful consideration of trade-offs between competing objectives. The Pareto front analysis provides decision-makers with a comprehensive set of non-dominated solutions, each representing different compromises between generation cost, transmission losses, voltage profile improvement, and system stability enhancement.

Pareto Dominance Definition:

Solution x₁ dominates solution x₂ if:

$$\forall i: f_i(x_1) \leq f_i(x_2) \text{ and } \exists j: f_j(x_1) < f_j(x_2)$$

8.2 Trade-off Analysis

The analysis reveals several key trade-offs:

  • Cost vs. Losses: Solutions minimizing generation cost may result in higher transmission losses
  • Voltage Profile vs. Stability: Optimal voltage profiles may compromise stability margins
  • Economic vs. Technical: Economic objectives often conflict with technical performance metrics
  • Short-term vs. Long-term: Immediate cost savings may affect long-term system reliability
