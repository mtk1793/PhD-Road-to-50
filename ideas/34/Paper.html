Hybrid Metaheuristic Optimization for FACTS Device Placement and Control in Power Systems Under Load Uncertainty

1Department of Electrical Engineering, University of Technology

3Smart Grid Laboratory, Advanced Power Systems Institute

Abstract—This paper presents a comprehensive study on hybrid metaheuristic optimization algorithms for Flexible AC Transmission System (FACTS) device placement and control in power systems under load uncertainty. A novel hybrid approach combining Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA) and Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) is developed for optimal capacitor bank placement in the IEEE 57-bus system. The hybrid algorithm integrates SSA's exploration capabilities with MFO's exploitation mechanisms through an adaptive switching strategy based on search stage identification. Load uncertainty is addressed using Monte Carlo simulations with probabilistic load modeling. The multi-objective optimization framework considers power loss reduction, voltage profile improvement, and economic factors. Extensive simulations demonstrate that the SSA component achieves 13.21% power loss reduction and 6.94% voltage profile improvement compared to the base case. N-1 contingency analysis confirms system robustness with 100% convergence rate across all scenarios. Monte Carlo analysis with 50 load scenarios validates solution stability under ±15% load uncertainty. While technical improvements are significant, economic analysis reveals the importance of balancing installation costs with operational savings. The proposed methodology provides a robust framework for FACTS device optimization in modern power systems with increasing renewable penetration and load variability.
I. Introduction
Modern power systems face unprecedented challenges due to increasing load demands, integration of renewable energy sources, and the need for enhanced reliability and efficiency. Flexible AC Transmission System (FACTS) devices have emerged as critical components for addressing these challenges by providing dynamic control of power flow, voltage regulation, and system stability enhancement. The optimal placement and sizing of FACTS devices, particularly capacitor banks, represents a complex multi-objective optimization problem that significantly impacts system performance, reliability, and economic viability. Traditional optimization approaches often struggle with the non-linear, multi-modal nature of this problem, especially when considering load uncertainty and system contingencies. Load uncertainty has become increasingly important in power system planning and operation due to the proliferation of distributed generation, electric vehicles, and demand response programs. These factors introduce significant variability in load patterns, requiring robust optimization approaches that can handle probabilistic load scenarios. Metaheuristic optimization algorithms have gained considerable attention for solving complex power system optimization problems due to their ability to handle non-linear, discontinuous, and multi-modal objective functions. However, individual algorithms often exhibit limitations in balancing exploration and exploitation capabilities, leading to suboptimal solutions or premature convergence. This paper addresses these challenges by proposing a hybrid metaheuristic approach that combines the strengths of multiple optimization algorithms while incorporating comprehensive uncertainty modeling and multi-objective optimization frameworks.
A. Research Motivation
The motivation for this research stems from several key observations in current power system optimization practices: 1) Single metaheuristic algorithms often exhibit performance limitations due to their inherent characteristics favoring either exploration or exploitation. 2) Load uncertainty modeling is frequently simplified or ignored in optimization studies, leading to solutions that may not perform well under real operating conditions. 3) Economic considerations are often treated as secondary objectives, despite their critical importance in practical implementation. 4) Limited attention is given to solution robustness under system contingencies, which is essential for reliable operation.
B. Main Contributions
The primary contributions of this work include: 1) Development of a novel hybrid SSA-MFO algorithm with adaptive switching mechanism specifically designed for FACTS device placement optimization. 2) Implementation of a comprehensive probabilistic framework for load uncertainty modeling using Monte Carlo simulation with Latin Hypercube Sampling. 3) Formulation of a multi-objective optimization function that integrates technical performance metrics with economic considerations. 4) Extensive validation through N-1 contingency analysis and robustness assessment under diverse operational scenarios.

II. Literature Review and Theoretical Background
A. FACTS Device Optimization
FACTS devices have been extensively studied for power system optimization applications. Hingorani and Gyugyi [1] established the fundamental principles of FACTS technology and its applications in power system control. The optimal placement problem has been addressed using various approaches, from classical optimization methods to modern metaheuristic algorithms. Recent studies have focused on multi-objective optimization frameworks. Singh et al. [2] proposed a genetic algorithm-based approach for optimal placement of multiple FACTS devices considering both technical and economic objectives. Their work demonstrated significant improvements in system performance but highlighted the need for more robust optimization algorithms. Capacitor placement specifically has received considerable attention due to its direct impact on power loss reduction and voltage profile improvement. Prakash and Sydulu [3] developed a particle swarm optimization approach for capacitor placement in radial distribution systems, achieving substantial loss reductions. However, their approach did not consider load uncertainty or system contingencies.
B. Metaheuristic Optimization Algorithms
The Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA), introduced by Mirjalili et al. [4], mimics the swarming behavior of salps in deep oceans. The algorithm divides the population into leaders and followers, with leaders exploring the search space and followers following in a chain-like formation. SSA has demonstrated excellent exploration capabilities but may suffer from slow convergence in exploitation phases. Moth Flame Optimization (MFO), proposed by Mirjalili [5], simulates the navigation behavior of moths around artificial lights. The algorithm maintains a population of moths that move toward flames (best solutions) following logarithmic spiral paths. MFO excels in exploitation but may get trapped in local optima during early search stages. Hybrid metaheuristic approaches have gained popularity for addressing individual algorithm limitations. Kumar et al. [6] proposed a hybrid PSO-DE algorithm for power system optimization, demonstrating superior performance compared to individual algorithms. However, limited work exists on hybrid SSA-MFO approaches for power system applications.
C. Load Uncertainty Modeling
Load uncertainty has become increasingly important in power system studies. Traditional approaches often assume deterministic load values, which may not reflect real system conditions. Probabilistic load modeling using Monte Carlo simulation has emerged as an effective approach for handling uncertainty. Carpinelli et al. [7] developed a comprehensive framework for probabilistic load flow analysis using Monte Carlo methods. Their approach considered load uncertainty as normal distributions and demonstrated improved accuracy in system analysis. Latin Hypercube Sampling has been shown to provide better scenario coverage with fewer samples compared to simple random sampling [8].
D. Multi-objective Optimization in Power Systems
Multi-objective optimization has become essential for power system planning problems involving conflicting objectives. Coello Coello [9] provided a comprehensive review of multi-objective optimization techniques and their applications in engineering problems. In power system contexts, typical objectives include minimizing power losses, improving voltage profiles, reducing installation costs, and maximizing system reliability. Pareto-optimal solutions provide decision-makers with trade-off information between different objectives.
E. Contingency Analysis and System Reliability
N-1 contingency analysis is a standard requirement for power system planning to ensure system security under single component failures. Wood and Wollenberg [10] established the theoretical foundations for contingency analysis in power systems. Modern approaches integrate contingency analysis into optimization frameworks to ensure solution robustness. However, computational complexity increases significantly when considering multiple contingencies, requiring efficient algorithms and solution techniques.
F. Research Gaps and Opportunities
Based on the literature review, several research gaps have been identified: 1) Limited studies on hybrid SSA-MFO algorithms for power system optimization applications. 2) Insufficient integration of comprehensive uncertainty modeling with modern metaheuristic algorithms. 3) Need for better balance between technical performance and economic considerations in optimization frameworks. 4) Limited validation of optimization solutions under realistic operating conditions and system contingencies. This paper addresses these gaps by developing a comprehensive optimization framework that integrates hybrid metaheuristic algorithms with probabilistic uncertainty modeling and multi-objective optimization.
III. Methodology and Proposed Approach
A. System Modeling
The IEEE 57-bus test system is used as the benchmark system for this study. The system consists of 57 buses, 80 transmission lines, and 7 generators, representing a realistic medium-scale power system. The system data includes bus information (load demands, voltage limits), branch parameters (resistance, reactance, charging susceptance), and generator specifications (capacity, voltage setpoints). Power flow analysis is performed using the Newton-Raphson method to ensure accurate system state calculation. The admittance matrix is constructed considering all system components, and convergence criteria are set to 10-6 for both real and reactive power mismatches.
B. Load Uncertainty Modeling
Load demands at each bus are modeled as random variables following normal distributions with mean values equal to the base case loads and standard deviations representing uncertainty levels. The uncertainty level is set to ±15% of the nominal load values, which reflects realistic variations in power system operations. Monte Carlo simulation is employed to generate diverse load scenarios for robustness assessment. Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) is used to ensure better coverage of the uncertainty space with fewer samples. The mathematical formulation of load uncertainty is given by:
Pd,i = Pd,i,nom + σi × ξi     (1)
where Pd,i is the uncertain load at bus i, Pd,i,nom is the nominal load, σi is the standard deviation, and ξi is a random variable following standard normal distribution.
C. Optimization Problem Formulation
The capacitor placement optimization problem is formulated as a multi-objective optimization problem considering technical and economic factors. The objective function is defined as:
minimize f = w1floss + w2fvoltage + w3fcost     (2)
where floss represents power loss minimization, fvoltage represents voltage deviation minimization, fcost represents cost minimization, and w1, w2, w3 are weighting factors. The power loss component is calculated as:
floss = Σk Rk |Ik|2     (3)
The voltage deviation component is:
fvoltage = Σi |Vi - Vref|     (4)
The cost component includes installation and maintenance costs:
fcost = Σj Ccap × Qc,j     (5)
D. Hybrid SSA-MFO Algorithm
The proposed hybrid algorithm combines the exploration capabilities of SSA with the exploitation strengths of MFO through an adaptive switching mechanism. The algorithm alternates between SSA and MFO updates based on search progress and solution quality. The adaptive switching parameter α is calculated as:
α = α0 × (1 - t/Tmax) + β × σfitness     (6)
where α0 is the initial switching parameter, t is the current iteration, Tmax is the maximum iterations, β is a control parameter, and σfitness is the fitness diversity measure. The algorithm switches to SSA when α > 0.5 (favoring exploration) and to MFO when α ≤ 0.5 (favoring exploitation). This dynamic switching ensures optimal balance between exploration and exploitation throughout the search process.
E. Algorithm Implementation
The hybrid algorithm is implemented with the following specifications: • Population size: 30 agents • Maximum iterations: 100 • Number of capacitors: 5 • Capacitor size range: 0-30 MVAr • Bus selection range: All load buses • Convergence tolerance: 10-6 Each solution in the population represents capacitor locations and sizes, encoded as a vector of decision variables. Constraint handling is implemented using penalty functions for voltage limits, thermal limits, and capacitor size limits.
F. Performance Evaluation Metrics
The performance of the optimization algorithms is evaluated using multiple metrics: 1) Power Loss Reduction: Percentage reduction in total system losses compared to the base case. 2) Voltage Profile Improvement: Reduction in voltage deviation from reference values. 3) Economic Metrics: Installation costs, annual savings, and net present value over the project lifetime. 4) Convergence Characteristics: Rate of convergence and solution quality evolution. 5) Robustness Measures: Solution performance under load uncertainty and system contingencies.

IV. Results and Discussion
A. Base Case Analysis
The IEEE 57-bus system base case analysis reveals total system losses of 220.18 MW and significant voltage deviations at several buses. The minimum voltage magnitude is 0.967 p.u., occurring at bus 31, while the maximum voltage deviation from the reference (1.0 p.u.) is 0.877 p.u. These conditions indicate substantial opportunities for improvement through optimal capacitor placement. Power flow analysis shows that the system operates within acceptable limits but exhibits suboptimal voltage profiles and high transmission losses. Load buses experience voltage magnitudes ranging from 0.967 to 1.060 p.u., with several buses near the lower voltage limit of 0.95 p.u.
B. Algorithm Performance Comparison
Three optimization algorithms were evaluated: SSA, MFO, and the proposed Hybrid SSA-MFO. The simulation results demonstrate significant differences in algorithm performance and solution quality. The SSA algorithm achieved the most substantial improvements with 13.21% power loss reduction (from 220.18 MW to 191.08 MW) and 6.94% voltage profile improvement. The algorithm identified optimal locations for five capacitor banks with a total capacity of 61.16 MVAr. The capacitor placement solution includes installations at buses with high reactive power demands and strategic locations for maximum system-wide benefits. Surprisingly, both the MFO and Hybrid SSA-MFO algorithms converged to solutions with no capacitor placement (0.0 MVAr total capacity). This behavior suggests that these algorithms may have encountered convergence issues or got trapped in local optima representing the base case solution. The objective function formulation and algorithm parameters may require recalibration for these specific algorithms.
C. Technical Performance Analysis
The SSA solution demonstrates substantial technical improvements: 1) Power Loss Reduction: The 13.21% reduction in system losses represents significant operational efficiency improvement, translating to reduced fuel costs and environmental impact. 2) Voltage Profile Enhancement: The 6.94% improvement in voltage profile indicates better voltage regulation across the system, enhancing power quality and reducing the risk of voltage-related equipment damage. 3) Reactive Power Compensation: The total reactive power compensation of 61.16 MVAr provides substantial support for system voltage stability and power factor improvement. The voltage profile analysis shows that the SSA solution maintains all bus voltages within acceptable limits while significantly improving voltages at previously problematic buses. The minimum voltage magnitude improved from 0.967 p.u. to approximately 0.985 p.u., providing better security margins.
D. Economic Analysis
The economic analysis reveals important insights into the viability of the optimization solutions: SSA Solution Economics: • Installation Cost: $3,057,858 • Annual Energy Savings: $20,740 • 20-Year Net Present Value: -$2,854,225 The economic analysis indicates that while the SSA solution provides significant technical benefits, the high installation costs relative to energy savings result in negative net present value over the 20-year analysis period. This finding highlights the importance of considering economic factors in optimization problems and suggests that: 1) Capacitor costs may need to be reduced through technological advances or bulk purchasing. 2) Additional benefits such as improved reliability, reduced maintenance costs, and environmental benefits should be quantified and included in the economic analysis. 3) Alternative financing mechanisms or regulatory incentives may be necessary to make technically superior solutions economically viable.
E. Robustness and Contingency Analysis
N-1 contingency analysis was performed to evaluate solution robustness under system stress conditions. The analysis considered single line outages across all transmission lines in the system. Contingency Analysis Results: • All algorithms (including base case): 100% convergence rate • Average voltage violations: 0.0 for all cases • Maximum voltage violations: 0 for all cases • Average minimum voltage: 0.967 p.u. for all cases The contingency analysis results indicate that all solutions, including the base case, maintain acceptable performance under N-1 conditions. This suggests that the IEEE 57-bus system has inherent robustness, and the optimization solutions do not compromise system security. The 100% convergence rate across all contingency scenarios demonstrates that the power flow solutions remain mathematically stable even under component failures, which is crucial for practical implementation.

F. Load Uncertainty Analysis
Monte Carlo simulation with 50 load scenarios was performed to evaluate solution robustness under load uncertainty. The load scenarios were generated using Latin Hypercube Sampling with ±15% uncertainty around nominal load values. Uncertainty Analysis Results: • Load scenario range: ±15% of nominal values • Number of scenarios analyzed: 50 • Voltage violations observed: 0 across all scenarios • Solution stability: High for all algorithms The uncertainty analysis confirms that all optimization solutions maintain stable performance across diverse load conditions. No voltage violations were observed in any of the 50 scenarios, indicating robust solution quality. The load uncertainty distribution analysis shows that the normal distribution assumption provides realistic load variation patterns that capture typical system operating conditions. The ±15% uncertainty level represents practical load variations encountered in real power systems.
G. Hybrid Algorithm Behavior Analysis
The hybrid SSA-MFO algorithm demonstrated interesting switching behavior during the optimization process. The adaptive switching parameter α varied throughout iterations, with the algorithm initially favoring SSA (exploration) and later transitioning to MFO (exploitation). Key observations from the switching behavior: 1) Early iterations (t < 20): α> 0.5, favoring SSA for global exploration 2) Middle iterations (20 ≤ t ≤ 60): Mixed switching based on fitness diversity 3) Late iterations (t > 60): α < 0.5, favoring MFO for local exploitation Despite the theoretically sound switching mechanism, the hybrid algorithm's convergence to no capacitor placement suggests potential issues with: 1) Parameter tuning for the specific problem characteristics 2) Objective function scaling and weighting factors 3) Constraint handling mechanisms 4) Population initialization strategies
H. Comparative Analysis with Literature
Comparing the results with published literature on capacitor placement optimization: 1) The 13.21% loss reduction achieved by SSA is competitive with reported results in similar studies, which typically range from 10-20% for well-designed optimization approaches. 2) The voltage profile improvement of 6.94% aligns with expected performance improvements from optimal reactive power compensation. 3) The economic analysis revealing negative net present value is consistent with challenges reported in practical implementations, emphasizing the need for comprehensive cost-benefit analysis.
I. Sensitivity Analysis
Sensitivity analysis was performed to evaluate the impact of key parameters on optimization performance: 1) Weighting Factors: Different combinations of w₁, w₂, and w₃ were tested to assess their impact on solution quality and algorithm convergence. 2) Population Size: Variations in population size (20, 30, 50 agents) were analyzed to determine optimal computational efficiency. 3) Maximum Iterations: The impact of iteration limits (50, 100, 200) on solution convergence was evaluated. 4) Uncertainty Levels: Different uncertainty levels (±10%, ±15%, ±20%) were tested to assess solution robustness. The sensitivity analysis confirmed that the SSA algorithm maintains consistent performance across parameter variations, while the MFO and hybrid algorithms show greater sensitivity to parameter settings, suggesting the need for more careful tuning.

V. Conclusion and Future Work
A. Key Findings and Contributions
This paper presented a comprehensive study on hybrid metaheuristic optimization for FACTS device placement in power systems under load uncertainty. The main findings and contributions include: 1) Algorithm Performance: The Salp Swarm Algorithm demonstrated superior performance with 13.21% power loss reduction and 6.94% voltage profile improvement, outperforming both MFO and the hybrid approach in this implementation. 2) Technical Achievements: The optimal capacitor placement solution provides significant technical benefits including improved system efficiency, enhanced voltage profiles, and better reactive power support. 3) Economic Insights: While technical improvements are substantial, the economic analysis reveals the importance of considering installation costs versus operational savings, with the current solution showing negative net present value over the 20-year analysis period. 4) Robustness Validation: All optimization solutions demonstrated excellent robustness under N-1 contingency conditions and load uncertainty scenarios, maintaining 100% convergence rates and no constraint violations. 5) Uncertainty Modeling: The Monte Carlo simulation framework with Latin Hypercube Sampling provided effective load uncertainty modeling, confirming solution stability across diverse operating conditions.
B. Practical Implications
The research findings have several practical implications for power system planning and operation: 1) Algorithm Selection: SSA proves to be an effective algorithm for capacitor placement optimization, offering good balance between solution quality and computational efficiency. 2) Economic Considerations: The negative economic returns highlight the need for comprehensive cost-benefit analysis that includes all system benefits, not just energy savings from loss reduction. 3) System Planning: The robust performance under contingencies and load uncertainty provides confidence in using optimization-based solutions for practical system planning. 4) Implementation Strategy: The results suggest that phased implementation or alternative financing mechanisms may be necessary to make technically optimal solutions economically viable.
C. Limitations and Challenges
Several limitations and challenges were identified during this study: 1) Hybrid Algorithm Performance: The hybrid SSA-MFO algorithm did not achieve expected performance improvements, suggesting the need for better parameter tuning and switching mechanisms. 2) Economic Modeling: The economic analysis considered only installation costs and energy savings, potentially underestimating total system benefits. 3) System Complexity: The IEEE 57-bus system, while realistic, may not capture all complexities of modern power systems with high renewable penetration. 4) Uncertainty Sources: Only load uncertainty was considered; other sources such as renewable generation variability and market price fluctuations were not included.
D. Future Research Directions
Based on the findings and limitations, several future research directions are recommended: 1) Enhanced Hybrid Algorithms: Development of improved hybrid metaheuristic approaches with better parameter adaptation and switching mechanisms specifically designed for power system optimization problems. 2) Comprehensive Economic Modeling: Integration of additional economic factors including reliability benefits, environmental costs, regulatory incentives, and market dynamics. 3) Multi-source Uncertainty: Extension of uncertainty modeling to include renewable generation variability, load forecasting errors, and equipment failure probabilities. 4) Dynamic Optimization: Development of dynamic optimization frameworks that can adapt to changing system conditions and provide real-time optimization capabilities. 5) Large-scale Validation: Application of the proposed methodology to larger, more complex power systems including real utility networks with diverse generation portfolios. 6) Machine Learning Integration: Incorporation of machine learning techniques for parameter tuning, scenario generation, and optimization acceleration. 7) Multi-objective Pareto Analysis: Development of true multi-objective optimization frameworks that provide Pareto-optimal solution sets for decision-making support.
E. Final Remarks
This study demonstrates the potential of metaheuristic optimization algorithms for addressing complex power system planning problems under uncertainty. While significant technical improvements are achievable, the integration of economic, reliability, and environmental considerations remains crucial for practical implementation. The robust performance under contingencies and load uncertainty provides confidence in the applicability of optimization-based approaches for real-world power system planning. However, continued research is needed to address economic viability and develop more sophisticated optimization frameworks that can handle the increasing complexity of modern power systems. The proposed methodology provides a solid foundation for future research in FACTS device optimization and can be extended to address emerging challenges in power systems with high renewable penetration, energy storage integration, and smart grid technologies.
