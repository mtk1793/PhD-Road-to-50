PARA_0: Neuro-OptimaFACTS: A Novel Explainable AI-Based Hybrid Framework for Optimized FACTS Device Control and Dynamic Voltage Regulation in Renewable-Integrated Grids \n\nPARA_2: Mahmoud M.Kiasari, Hamed Aly \n\nPARA_3: Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Dalhousie University, Halifax, Canada \n\nPARA_5: The stability of the grid and its efficient operation is an important challenge and as the modern power system adopts renewable sources of energy, the challenge remains to be one important one. Flexible AC Transmission System (FACTS) technologies, such as static synchronous compensator (STATCOM), SVC, and UPFC, are central to maintaining voltage stability, improving power quality, and providing reactive power compensation. However, their optimal tuning and predictive maintenance remain complex tasks owing to the nonlinear and dynamic nature of power networks. This study proposes the development of a new technique, named Neuro-OptimaFACTS, which is a hybrid structure that utilizes Wavelet Neural Networks (WNN), Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Recurrent Kalman Filters (RKF), and Fourier Series (FS) to meet such challenges. The framework uses explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) using SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations), which provides a window into the system decisions. It also consists of residual-based energy estimation and tunable voltage control to make the system more responsiveResults obtained on a real-time IEEE 39-bus New England dataset representing the northeastern United States, built from integrated NOAA weather, NREL renewable generation, and EIA load measurements, show that Neuro-OptimaFACTS constrains bus voltages to approximately 0.99–1.01 p.u. (about ±1% of nominal), reduces effective harmonic content relative to PI by 1.56×, improves frequency stability by 2.23×, and delivers 1.76× higher overall grid-support effectiveness than traditional control.\n\nPARA_6: Keywords: Electric vehicle, state of charge prediction, machine learning, neural networks, battery management\n\nPARA_7: Background and Motivation\n\nPARA_8: Managing systems Modern transmission systems are already working closer than ever to their limits, and non-synchronous renewables are presenting fast, stochastic variability, which is pushing the limits of voltage stability and quality of power and to the balance of reactive power. The concept of a Flexible AC Transmission System to achieve careful, adjustable voltage support and power-flow control under these operating conditions spawned FACTS devices, most famously the STATCOM, SVC, and UPFC, which became the prototypical toolkit of dynamic reactionary power management of high-voltage grids[1].\n\nPARA_9: System inertia reductions with inverter-based characteristics instead of synchronous machines lead to the acceleration of the dynamics of frequency and voltage and make classic single-objective controllers (such as fixed-gain PI or modal controllers tuned only to a limited range of contingencies) progressively fragile. New research categorizes the degree to which decreased inertia narrows time constants and tremors the viability of older control approaches amid perturbations that often resemble renewable-rich systems. Simultaneously, IEEE Std 519 continues to characterize regulatory requirements of compliance with power quality based on the parameter of maximum harmonic distortion in total harmonic distortion occurring at the point of common coupling, which poses explicit limitations on allowable total harmonic distortion, as well as requires utilities to measure, analyze, and minimize distortion under varied loading conditions[2], [3]. These convergent trends encourage the creation of controllers that:\n\nPARA_10: React on sub-cycle reactions, sub-second time scales, over a broad operating envelope, \n\nPARA_11: Discover simultaneous optimization of performance response (voltage profile, THD, reactive support, damping)\n\nPARA_12: Their rationale is reported in a revelatory manner, enabling engineers to justify their actions in compliance and safety regimes.\n\nPARA_13: Artificial intelligence (AI) has proven capable of guiding the forecast, control, and diagnostics of power systems; however, dense models cannot be relied on by equipment operating under essential grid challenges. Explainable AI (XAI) frameworks such as SHAP (which provides feature-level contributions based on the values of Shapley) or LIME (which provides local surrogate models) provide a channel for transparent decision support auditable by grid operators. In the area of power and energy, recent surveys highlight both the opportunities and multiple challenges involved in realizing the use of XAI in operational decision-making processes, thus emphasizing the need for interpretable pipelines that would smoothly interact with operational protection and compliance processes[4], [5], [6].\n\nPARA_14: Signal processing and estimation frameworks are inseparable from the learning process. Wavelet-based decompositions suppress non-stationary power-quality bursts, thereby enhancing the timing of control responses, and state estimation formalisms, such as Kalman-filter variants, synthesize noisy, asynchronous measurements into consistent system states to be fed back. Taken together, these aspects imply a hybrid paradigm of control comprising more experienced learners to discover certain patterns, principled estimators, and physics-conscious modules and overlaying an XAI layer at the top, thus making actions both effective and explainable[7], [8].\n\nPARA_15: Research Gaps\n\nPARA_16: Despite substantial progress, four gaps persist.\n\nPARA_17: Single-method controller’ domination: Many FACTS control studies only apply one paradigm, such as artificial neural networks, fuzzy logic, or genetic algorithms/particle swarm optimization, thus limiting their flexibility in two timescales of complex hard-to-model nonlinear dynamics found in renewable-integrated power systems. Coordinated multi-module hybrid architectures, simultaneous in both time and frequency decomposition, and nonlinear mapping followed by state estimation are elusive in the field of operations[9].\n\nPARA_18: Limited Explainability for Real-time FACTS Actions: Real-time FACTS actions can be limited in terms of explainability. Although XAI tools have been widely studied, their role as part of closed-loop FACTS control, where an explanation is required for every action in line with regulatory limitations, has not been explored much[6].\n\nPARA_19: Insufficient Multi-objective Optimization: The current literature largely encompasses the optimization of voltage regulation or reactive support on a case-by-case basis; relatively few control systems are designed to minimize overall harmonic distortion (THD) in parallel with ensuring volume-stability margins and imposing power-factor requirements on a range of devices, including STATCOM, SVC, and UPFC. As a result, controllers produced through these studies tend to reach reasonable performance measures in a single measure at the cost of compromising the performance value of other measures[10].\n\nPARA_20: Scaling from Small Cases to Realistic Grids: Controllers tested on small networks often do not scale to meshed transmission systems such as the IEEE 39 Bus and New England test systems, which capture realistic interactions and stress patterns. At this level, methodologies that extrapolate over contingencies and renewable generation profiles are still required to be developed.\n\nPARA_21: Research Contribution and Novelty\n\nPARA_22: To address the outlined inadequacies, we propose Neuro-OptimaFACTS, an explainable built-in control architecture that aids the distributed execution of STATCOM, SVC, and UPFC amid renewable-filled transmission systems. The architecture combines \n\nPARA_23: A wavelet-based outbreak of time on localisation of perturbation, \n\nPARA_24: Neural networks to map non-linear religion laws, \n\nPARA_25: State estimation and data reconciliation through a recurrent Kalman-filtering module\n\nPARA_26: Fourier-series/THD monitoring in accordance with the accuracy of IEEE-519 standards, complemented by the augmentation of an SHAP-based XAI layer that explains the factors behind each decision on the control decision.\n\nPARA_27: The proposed methodology is validated on the IEEE 39-bus New England system using a real-time dataset for the northeastern United States, constructed from NOAA weather, NREL wind/solar generation, and EIA load data mapped to the network topology. Extensive simulation work has shown that the controller maintains bus voltages within defined operational limits, inventively combats harmonic distortion, and augmentatively supports reactive power of strong virtual schemes. \n\nPARA_28: The contributions of this study are as follows:\n\nPARA_29: Hybrid physics‑aware controller. This single design combines the implementation of wavelet signal decomposition and nonlinear policy using artificial neural networks, as well as dynamic Kalman filtering, which performs accurately under the conditions of noise and time-varying[7], [8].  \n\nPARA_30: Explainable FACTS decision-making. A SHAP-based attribution layer supplies actionable, per-decision explanations that enhance operator trust, facilitate auditing, and enable tuning[4].  \n\nPARA_31: Multi-objective optimization in compliance with standards. This structure also aligns with voltage regulation and power quality, such as total harmonic distortion (THD), optimized according to IEEE guidelines 519[11].  \n\nPARA_32: Scalable validation. This was implemented by integrating WNN, ANN, RKF, and FS in a smooth manner to enable the hybrid model to perform optimally relative to all the relevant parameters: 97.3 percent voltage regulation, 94.8 percent THD reduction, and 92.1 percent reactive power rule enhancement. With increased explainability promoted by SHAP analysis and an explanation accuracy of 94.6 %, operators will have confidence in and be able to understand system decisions. The suggested framework has significant potential for application in a wide variety of real-life utility and renewable settings. In utility-scale systems, Neuro-Optima FACTS may be used in the fields of dynamic voltage control, enhancement of power quality, grid stability, and predictive maintenance of FACTS infrastructure. The model is well-adapted to coordinated control in wind and solar farms, energy storage systems, and microgrid operations in the renewable sector. Its efficiency in handling non-linearities and grid fluctuations makes it invaluable in smart-grid settings, where rapid, trustworthy, and interpretable decisions continue to be the key factors for maintaining resilience and efficiency.\n\nPARA_213: Despite the admirable features of Neuro-Optima FACTS, multiple drawbacks must be overcome to foster its scalability and adoption. Challenges include the computational expense in very large-scale systems, trustworthy performance across diverse working environments, reduced reliance on huge training information and precise system specifications, and the application of this AI-based structure into a working setting with consideration of regulatory requirements and system combination. Future Directions will involve the use of advanced architecture, including transformers and graph neural networks, federated learning to facilitate secure multi-utility communications, and optimization of edge computing to decrease latency. To further assure secure and safe operation, it is essential to strengthen the cyber resistance of the framework by creating adversarial training and resilient control measures. These guidelines will guide Neuro-Optima FACTS to become a disruptive action in the development of smart, green, and intelligent power grids. To ensure the framework’s scalability for utility-scale grids, upcoming research will focus on deploying Neuro-Optima FACTS in larger, multi-area transmission networks and integrating with legacy automation platforms.
