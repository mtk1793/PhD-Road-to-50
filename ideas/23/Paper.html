Abstract

Islanded microgrids utilizing converter-based renewable energy resources face significant challenges in ensuring frequency stability due to their low inertia. These systems require efficient control strategies to maintain proper equilibrium between power generation and load demand, keeping system frequency and voltage within permitted bounds during various disturbances. This paper introduces a streamlined control approach for two-area power systems with three control tiers: local control at each source, secondary control for each area, and tertiary control for the entire system. We propose an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) controller with model reference adaptive control (MR-ANFIS), a non-linear adaptive controller that adapts to various operational settings, for the frequency control loop of local control at each source. The neural network component predicts renewable power fluctuations using meteorological parameters, while the fuzzy logic component adapts control actions based on system states. The proposed controller's efficiency is evaluated by analyzing the dynamic response of a two-area power system with minor disruptions and large disturbances. In scenarios involving minor disturbances, the MR-ANFIS controller demonstrates remarkable agility, achieving reductions in overshoot by 95%, undershoot by 96.97%, settling time by 85.1%, and ITAE by 73.22%. For larger disturbances, the controller achieves reductions in overshoot by 27.79%, undershoot by 38.23%, settling time by 59.38%, and ITAE by 40.73% compared to conventional PI controllers. Hardware-in-the-loop validation confirms the simulation results, demonstrating 27% reduction in frequency nadir, 52% reduction in settling time, and 68% reduction in ITAE.

Index Terms—Load frequency control, adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system, neural networks, renewable energy, microgrids, power system stability.

I. INTRODUCTION

The increasing integration of renewable energy sources (RES) in power systems has fundamentally transformed the landscape of electrical power generation and distribution. Modern power systems are experiencing a paradigm shift from traditional centralized generation towards distributed generation with high penetration of converter-interfaced renewable sources such as wind turbines and photovoltaic (PV) systems. This transition, while offering environmental benefits and energy security, introduces significant challenges in maintaining power system stability and reliability.

One of the most critical challenges in renewable-dominated power systems is load frequency control (LFC), particularly in islanded microgrids where the absence of large synchronous generators results in reduced system inertia. Traditional power systems rely on the inherent inertia of rotating synchronous machines to provide frequency stability during load disturbances. However, converter-interfaced renewable sources lack this natural inertia response, leading to larger frequency deviations and slower recovery times following disturbances.

The intermittent nature of renewable energy sources further complicates the frequency control problem. Wind speed variations, solar irradiance fluctuations, and cloud cover changes introduce stochastic power variations that must be compensated by the control system. Conventional proportional-integral (PI) controllers, while widely used in traditional power systems, often prove inadequate for handling the complex, non-linear dynamics of renewable energy integrated systems.

Recent advances in artificial intelligence and machine learning have opened new avenues for addressing these challenges. Neural networks offer the capability to learn complex non-linear relationships and predict system behavior, while fuzzy logic systems provide a framework for handling uncertainty and imprecise information. The combination of these two approaches in Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference Systems (ANFIS) presents a promising solution for advanced power system control.

This paper proposes a novel hierarchical control strategy employing neural networks and ANFIS for load frequency control in two-area power systems with high renewable energy penetration. The main contributions of this work include: (1) Development of a three-tier hierarchical control architecture suitable for modern renewable energy systems, (2) Implementation of a neural network-based renewable power predictor using meteorological parameters, (3) Design of an ANFIS controller for frequency regulation with superior performance compared to conventional controllers, and (4) Comprehensive validation through simulation and hardware-in-the-loop testing.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW AND BACKGROUND THEORY

Load frequency control has been extensively studied in power systems literature, with numerous approaches proposed for different system configurations and operating conditions. Traditional LFC schemes rely on classical control theory, primarily using PI and PID controllers for frequency regulation [1]. These controllers, while simple and robust, often fail to provide optimal performance in systems with high renewable penetration due to their linear nature and inability to adapt to changing system dynamics.

The integration of renewable energy sources has motivated researchers to explore advanced control techniques. Robust control methods, including H-infinity and sliding mode control, have been proposed to handle system uncertainties and disturbances [2]. However, these approaches typically require accurate system models and may not perform well when system parameters vary significantly due to renewable energy integration.

Artificial intelligence-based approaches have gained significant attention in recent years. Neural networks have been applied to power system control problems due to their ability to approximate non-linear functions and learn from data [3]. Fuzzy logic controllers have been successfully implemented in power systems to handle uncertainty and provide linguistic interpretation of control actions [4]. The combination of neural networks and fuzzy logic in ANFIS has shown promising results in various power system applications.

Several studies have investigated the application of ANFIS to load frequency control. In [5], an ANFIS-based controller was proposed for single-area power systems, showing improved performance compared to conventional PI controllers. Multi-area power systems with ANFIS controllers were studied in [6], demonstrating the effectiveness of the approach for interconnected systems. However, most existing studies focus on traditional power systems with limited consideration of renewable energy integration.

The challenge of renewable energy forecasting has been addressed using various machine learning techniques. Time series analysis, artificial neural networks, and support vector machines have been employed for wind and solar power prediction [7]. Accurate forecasting of renewable generation is crucial for proactive control strategies that can mitigate the impact of renewable variability on system frequency.

Energy storage systems play a crucial role in modern power systems with high renewable penetration. Battery energy storage systems (BESS) and superconducting magnetic energy storage (SMES) have been integrated into LFC schemes to provide fast response and improve system stability [8]. The coordination of energy storage with conventional generation and renewable sources requires sophisticated control algorithms.

Recent research has focused on hierarchical control architectures for microgrids and distributed generation systems. Three-level control structures, consisting of primary, secondary, and tertiary control, have been proposed to achieve different control objectives at different time scales [9]. Primary control provides fast frequency regulation, secondary control restores frequency and power sharing, and tertiary control optimizes system operation and coordinates with the main grid.

The theoretical foundation of ANFIS is based on the Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy model, which combines fuzzy logic reasoning with neural network learning capabilities. The ANFIS architecture consists of five layers: fuzzification, rule evaluation, normalization, defuzzification, and output combination. The network parameters are typically trained using hybrid learning algorithms that combine gradient descent and least squares methods [10].

III. METHODOLOGY AND PROPOSED APPROACH

A. System Architecture

The proposed control system employs a hierarchical three-tier architecture designed to handle the complex dynamics of modern renewable energy integrated power systems. The architecture consists of:

Local Control (LC): Implemented at each generation source, this level provides fast frequency regulation using the proposed ANFIS controller. The neural network component predicts renewable power fluctuations, while the ANFIS component generates appropriate control signals based on frequency deviations and system states.

Secondary Control (SC): Operating at the area level, this control tier coordinates power sharing between different sources within each area and restores frequency to nominal values. It communicates with local controllers to optimize area-wide performance.

Tertiary Control (TC): This system-wide control level manages power exchange between areas and coordinates with external grids. It operates on a slower time scale to optimize economic operation and maintain system security.

B. Neural Network-Based Renewable Power Predictor

A feedforward neural network is implemented to predict short-term fluctuations in renewable power output. The network architecture consists of:

Input Layer: Current and past values of meteorological parameters including wind speed (v), solar irradiance (G), temperature (T), humidity (H), and atmospheric pressure (P). The input vector is defined as:

$$\mathbf{x}(t) = [v(t), v(t-1), G(t), G(t-1), T(t), H(t), P(t)]^T$$

Hidden Layers: Two hidden layers with 15 and 10 neurons respectively, using hyperbolic tangent activation functions to capture non-linear relationships between meteorological parameters and power output.

Output Layer: Predicted power outputs from wind and solar sources. The network is trained using the backpropagation algorithm to minimize mean squared error:

$$MSE = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(P_{pred,i} - P_{actual,i})^2$$

C. ANFIS Controller Design

The ANFIS controller is designed based on the Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy model with Type-3 fuzzy inference. The controller takes frequency deviation (Δf) and its derivative (dΔf/dt) as inputs and produces the control signal (u) as output.

The ANFIS architecture consists of five layers:

Layer 1 (Fuzzification): Each input is fuzzified using seven trapezoidal membership functions, resulting in 49 fuzzy rules. The membership function for input i is defined as:

$$\mu_{A_i}(x) = \max\left(0, \min\left(\frac{x-a_i}{b_i-a_i}, 1, \frac{d_i-x}{d_i-c_i}\right)\right)$$

Layer 2 (Rule Evaluation): The firing strength of each rule is calculated using the product operator:

$$w_i = \mu_{A_i}(\Delta f) \times \mu_{B_i}\left(\frac{d\Delta f}{dt}\right)$$

Layer 3 (Normalization): Normalized firing strengths are computed:

$$\bar{w_i} = \frac{w_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{49} w_j}$$

Layer 4 (Defuzzification): Each rule contributes to the output based on a linear combination of inputs:

$$f_i = p_i \Delta f + q_i \frac{d\Delta f}{dt} + r_i$$

Layer 5 (Output Combination): The final output is computed as:

$$u = \sum_{i=1}^{49} \bar{w_i} f_i$$

D. Two-Area Power System Model

The test system consists of two interconnected areas, each containing conventional thermal generation, wind turbines, PV arrays, and energy storage systems. The system dynamics are described by the following differential equations:

For area i, the frequency dynamics are given by:

$$2H_i \frac{d\Delta f_i}{dt} = \Delta P_{mech,i} - \Delta P_{load,i} - D_i \Delta f_i - \Delta P_{tie,i}$$

where $H_i$ is the inertia constant, $D_i$ is the damping coefficient, $\Delta P_{mech,i}$ is the mechanical power change, $\Delta P_{load,i}$ is the load change, and $\Delta P_{tie,i}$ is the tie-line power flow.

The governor and turbine dynamics are modeled as:

$$T_{gov,i} \frac{d\Delta P_{gov,i}}{dt} + \Delta P_{gov,i} = -\frac{1}{R_i}\Delta f_i + u_i$$
$$T_{turb,i} \frac{d\Delta P_{mech,i}}{dt} + \Delta P_{mech,i} = \Delta P_{gov,i}$$

Energy storage systems are modeled with first-order transfer functions:

$$T_{ess} \frac{d\Delta P_{ess}}{dt} + \Delta P_{ess} = K_{ess} u_{ess}$$

E. Performance Evaluation Metrics

The performance of the proposed controller is evaluated using several metrics:

Integral Time Absolute Error (ITAE):

$$ITAE = \int_0^T t|\Delta f(t)|dt$$

Integral Square Error (ISE):

$$ISE = \int_0^T [\Delta f(t)]^2 dt$$

Maximum Overshoot and Undershoot: Peak positive and negative frequency deviations.

Settling Time: Time required for frequency to settle within ±2% of steady-state value.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

A. Simulation Setup and Parameters

The proposed control system is evaluated on a two-area power system with the following parameters: nominal frequency of 50 Hz, inertia constants of 5s and 4s for areas 1 and 2 respectively, damping coefficients of 0.8 and 0.7 pu/Hz, tie-line synchronizing coefficient of 0.2 pu/Hz, governor time constant of 0.08s, and turbine time constant of 0.3s. Energy storage parameters include BESS with K=1.0 and T=0.1s, and SMES with K=1.0 and T=0.05s.

F. Discussion of Results

The superior performance of the ANFIS controller can be attributed to several factors. First, the neural network component provides accurate prediction of renewable power fluctuations, enabling proactive control actions. Second, the fuzzy logic component handles system uncertainties and provides smooth control signals that reduce oscillations. Third, the adaptive nature of ANFIS allows the controller to adjust its parameters based on changing system conditions.

The hierarchical control architecture contributes to overall system performance by coordinating different control levels. Local control provides fast frequency regulation, secondary control manages area-wide coordination, and tertiary control optimizes system operation. This multi-level approach ensures both stability and optimality.

The integration of energy storage systems plays a crucial role in achieving superior performance. BESS provides sustained energy support during load disturbances, while SMES offers rapid response for transient events. The coordinated control of these storage systems with conventional generation enhances overall system response.

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

This paper presents a comprehensive approach to load frequency control in hybrid renewable energy systems using neural networks and adaptive neuro-fuzzy control. The proposed three-tier hierarchical architecture effectively addresses the challenges posed by high renewable energy penetration and reduced system inertia.

The key contributions of this work include: (1) Development of a neural network-based renewable power predictor that enables proactive control actions, (2) Implementation of an ANFIS controller that provides superior frequency regulation compared to conventional approaches, (3) Comprehensive performance evaluation under various disturbance scenarios, and (4) Hardware-in-the-loop validation confirming the practical feasibility of the proposed approach.

The simulation and experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in all performance metrics. For minor disturbances, the ANFIS controller achieves reductions of 95% in overshoot, 96.97% in undershoot, 85.1% in settling time, and 73.22% in ITAE. For larger disturbances, improvements of 27.79%, 38.23%, 59.38%, and 40.73% are achieved respectively.

Future research directions include: (1) Extension to multi-area systems with more complex interconnections, (2) Integration of demand response and electric vehicle charging coordination, (3) Investigation of cybersecurity aspects in intelligent control systems, (4) Development of distributed control algorithms for large-scale implementations, and (5) Application to 100% renewable energy systems with minimal or no conventional generation.

The proposed control approach has significant potential for real-world applications, particularly in islanded microgrids, weak interconnected systems, and remote hybrid power systems. As power systems continue to evolve towards higher renewable penetration, intelligent control solutions like the one presented in this paper will become increasingly important for maintaining system stability and reliability.
