# Abstract

HQI-SAC-Fed (Hybrid Q-Informed Soft Actor-Critic with Federated Averaging) is presented in this paper as a provincial-scale federated deep reinforcement learning framework for coordinated battery energy storage system (BESS) dispatch across competing utility operators in a PIPEDA-regulated maritime wind grid. The proposed framework coordinates 520 MWh of distributed BESS across three Nova Scotia offshore wind sites—Guysborough (200 MWh), Halifax (120 MWh), and Cape Breton (200 MWh)—without any raw operational data exchange between utility operators. Four principal innovations are integrated: (i) Soft Actor-Critic agents augmented with hybrid Q-guidance (β=0.10) are employed to accelerate convergence and prevent reward hacking; (ii) admittance-weighted two-layer graph convolutional networks (GCN) are used to encode inter-site electrical coupling through the grid admittance matrix; (iii) capacity-weighted federated averaging is applied for privacy-preserving model aggregation; and (iv) Gaussian mechanism (ε,δ)-differential privacy (ε=1.0, δ=10⁻⁵) is incorporated to provide formal gradient inversion protection.

Calibrated on 16,444,284 records spanning seven real-world datasets (2022–2025), HQI-SAC-Fed is shown to achieve 97.1% of centralized performance (NPV: $13.2M vs. $13.6M CAD over a 15-year horizon) while reducing provincial wind curtailment from approximately 32% to 8.3% (−23.7 percentage points), avoiding 162 kt CO₂/year. The data sovereignty premium is found to be only 2.9% NPV ($0.4M), compared to a coordination gain of $3.2M over independent operation—an 8× benefit-to-cost ratio. Gradient inversion reconstruction MSE of 0.87 (above the 0.5 noise-dominance threshold) and membership inference attack success rate of 54.3% (near the 50% random baseline) confirm empirical privacy protection.

Statistical validation across 20 independent seeds establishes significance versus independent baselines (p=2.1×10⁻¹¹, Cohen's d=4.67) and practical equivalence to the centralized upper bound (p=0.48, t(38)=0.71). Proposition 1 formalizes convergence under the SCAFFOLD framework, analytically deriving the 18-round DP-induced delay and the $0.4M sovereignty premium as consequences of the ε=1.0 privacy budget.
